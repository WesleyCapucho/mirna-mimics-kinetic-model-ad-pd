# Methods

## 1. Mechanistic layer: ODE models and structural figures

This work asks something narrower than the companion systematic review and meta-analysis (10.5281/zenodo.23438256), and it should be read that way. It does not test whether a miRNA works as a biomarker. It asks what the measured kinetics of each axis allow, and where a claim about them outruns the numbers that exist.

**Where the parameters come from.** Every rate, half-life and concentration used by `scripts/12_ode_models_calibrated.py` is loaded by identifier from `data/extracted/kinetic_parameters.csv` (67 rows, 15 primary sources). Values were read from full texts or from supplementary files, never from abstracts or from secondary citations, and each numeric row stores the sentence it came from. The table separates four kinds of row: measured numbers, qualitative constraints (a result stated without a usable number, such as nucleation being undetectable at neutral pH), declared gaps, and values derived by us from a primary table. The loader refuses anything that is not a measured number, so a gap cannot be filled silently.

**What is measured and what is not.** Aβ42 production and clearance come from stable-isotope labelling kinetics in human CSF (Mawuenyega et al. 2010, DOI 10.1126/science.1197623). Aggregation exponents and the critical fibril concentration come from Cohen et al. 2013 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1218402110), and human brain Aβ42 loads from its Table S2. α-synuclein elongation and its pH dependence come from Buell et al. 2014 (DOI 10.1073/pnas.1315346111). miRNA half-lives come from Zhang et al. 2011, Gantier et al. 2011, Kingston and Bartel 2019 and Kleaveland et al. 2018, and protein turnover from Li et al. 2004, Liu et al. 2019 and Fornasiero et al. 2018. Aggregation rate constants that no source gives as a number (Aβ42 nucleation and elongation, α-synuclein nucleation at acidic pH) are fixed at declared **illustrative** values, listed with their reasons in the output; no reported finding depends on them. Two further quantities were never measured for these genes and are left **free**: the strength of miRNA repression and the translation rate. Each is scanned over a declared range on a geometric grid; neither is tuned to reach a result.

mRNA decay used to be a third free parameter and is not any more. Tushev et al. 2018 report it per 3'UTR isoform in cultured rat hippocampal neurons: SNCA has one isoform at 6.53 h (K066), while BACE1 has five, with half-lives from 2.8 to 23.9 h (K067). A pool of isoforms does not decay with the mean of their half-lives, so the BACE1 rate is the abundance-weighted mean of the **rate constants**, 0.0456 h⁻¹, an effective half-life of 15.2 h (K068); the mean of the half-lives would give 17.4 h, which is the wrong quantity. Two cautions came out of reading that table. Its decay was observed over 16 h, so the APP half-life of 51.4 h (K069) is an extrapolation and is recorded with a warning rather than used. And the paper's own neuron-enriched summary reproduces from the table only when half-lives above about 25 h are excluded (median 7.394 h against the printed 7.38), while the glia summary does not reproduce under any filter tried — so K044 is quoted as printed and never recomputed.

**Experiments run.** (i) The AD arm compares a 30% clearance deficit with the measured 1.5% production difference, and computes how far BACE1 must fall to offset the clearance deficit and the miR-29 mimic factor that delivers that fall. (ii) Single-dose mimic washout: the time for a 10-fold bolus to decay within 10% of baseline at each measured half-life. (iii) Human brain Aβ42 loads expressed as multiples of the critical concentration above which secondary nucleation dominates. (iv) The α-synuclein arm with nucleation switched off at neutral pH, where Buell et al. found it undetectable (K011), and on below pH 6, where they report that secondary nucleation "increases dramatically" (K042). The source gives the direction of that switch, not its size, so the acidic-pH rate constant is swept over four orders of magnitude; the fold change in fibril number then ranges from about 500 to about 14,000, and only the direction is reported as a result. (v) The miR-29 dose the clearance experiment requires, set beside a measured effect: Hébert et al. 2008 report about 50% BACE1 knockdown on miR-29a/b-1 transfection in SK-N-SH cells (K047). At steady state the BACE1 level is analytic in the mimic factor, so the required knockdown (1 − 1/R, with R the AD over control ratio of the monomer pool: 29.2%, the same at every value of the free parameters), the mimic factor that delivers it (1.42 at the midpoint of the repression strength, 1.41 to 1.48 over its declared range) and the mimic factor that would reproduce the measured 50% (2.0 to 2.2) are all computed in closed form, and the first two are checked by integrating the ODE (relative error below 10⁻⁹). An earlier version found the mimic factor by a grid search with a step of 0.247, which overshot and reported the required knockdown as a range of 30–33%; the exact value is 29.2%. (vi) Two calculations that use no free parameter at all, because they combine measured quantities directly. The α-synuclein concentration in a presynaptic bouton, 21.6 µM (derived from the combined synuclein copy number and the measured α:β ratio of 0.98:1, Wilhelm et al. 2014 table S1), is placed on the measured elongation saturation curve, whose half-maximal concentration is 46–50 µM (Buell et al. 2014): the protein sits at 30–32% of maximal elongation rate, below half-saturation, where a 1% fall in concentration buys a 0.68–0.70% fall in elongation rate. The same curve then carries a measured knockdown through to a rate: miR-7 lowers α-synuclein by 30% from an SNCA construct that carries its 3'UTR in HEK-293 cells and miR-7 with miR-153 lowers endogenous α-synuclein by 43% in rat cortical neurons (Doxakis 2010), which on the saturation curve become a 23% and a 34% slower elongation — less than proportional, because the curve saturates. Separately, BACE1 and APP are counted in the same preparation, 116 against 6284 copies per bouton. Both cross systems — an in vitro saturation constant against a rat synaptosome — and are reported as statements about regime and stoichiometry, not as rates. (vii) A scan of the free parameters, with every run classed as AD above control, at or below control, or not evaluable. A run that fails numerically is reported as not evaluable, never counted as a reversal. The scans integrate the monomer cascade only: the illustrative aggregation block feeds nothing back to it and had diverged at two corners of the scan in an earlier version, which were then reported as not evaluable; all 18 runs are now evaluable. Integration uses LSODA (`scipy.integrate.solve_ivp`, rtol 1e-8, atol 1e-10).

**What the models can and cannot claim.** The AD/control monomer ratio (1.41) equals the ratio of production to clearance measured by Mawuenyega et al. and is independent of every free parameter. The model restates that measurement; it does not predict it. The value of the models is elsewhere: they show that miR-29 paralogues decay at different rates (7 h and 10.6 h, one reported as stable) and cannot be treated as one species, that a single miR-7 mimic dose would be back near baseline within 11 to 32 hours against about 9 days for a typical miRNA, that the BACE1 knockdown needed to offset the measured clearance deficit is 29.2%, the same at every value of the free parameters and about 58% of the roughly 50% already achieved in cells, that a measured miR-7 knockdown of α-synuclein translates into a 23–34% slower fibril elongation through three independent measurements and no free parameter, and that the Aβ42 rate constants needed to go further cannot be separated with the published data (row K037).

**What repeated dosing costs a fast-turning-over mimic.** `scripts/17_mimic_dosing_feasibility.py` asks a question the washout calculation raises but does not answer: if a mimic has to be given repeatedly rather than once, what does the measured half-life cost? At steady state under repeated dosing of a species cleared with first-order constant *d* at interval *T*, the ratio of peak to average concentration is *dT* / (1 − e^(−*dT*)). The dose cancels, so the calculation has no free parameter at all; only the measured decay constants enter, loaded by identifier from the kinetic table.

**What repeated dosing does at the target.** `scripts/36_mimic_target_response.py` asks what `scripts/12` and `scripts/17` leave open: whether a regimen that holds the average mimic level also holds the average protein level. The mRNA and the protein are filters with their own half-lives, and repression is not linear in the miRNA, so it need not. The cascade (mimic excess, mRNA, protein, and in the AD arm the Aβ42 monomer) is written relative to the untreated steady state, so the translation rate divides out and the repression strength enters only through ρ, the decay that the miRNA adds to the mRNA at baseline in units of the mRNA's intrinsic decay (3k/d for the three miR-29 paralogues, k/d for miR-7; the miRNA then carries the fraction ρ/(1 + ρ) of the mRNA's total decay). Every other constant is one the earlier scripts load: mRNA decay (K068, K066), protein decay (K025, K026, and the in vivo brain proteome median K029 as a sensitivity case), Aβ42 clearance in AD (K004) and the decay of the delivered species (K016 to K021). For each scenario (axis, repression strength, delivered species, dosing interval, protein half-life, target) the script finds the bolus dose that brings the cycle-averaged protein down by the required knockdown at the periodic steady state of repeated dosing. The periodic state is exact (the block is linear, so it is a small linear system) and the cycle averages are integrated as states. Six independent checks must pass before a table is written: the linear limit, the peak-to-mean ratio against `scripts/17`, quasi-continuous dosing against the closed form, brute-force simulation over 120 cycles, constant exposure through `scripts/12`'s own ODE, and the identity of the two closed forms. Two exact properties follow and are enforced by `scripts/08`: over a periodic cycle the mean protein equals the mean mRNA, so the dose does not depend on the protein half-life, which sets only how far the protein swings; and the same holds for the Aβ42 monomer.

The repression strength ρ is the one free quantity left, and the parameter table holds one observation that bears on it, from below only: blocking endogenous miR-7 and miR-153 with antisense oligonucleotides raised an SNCA 3'UTR reporter by 44% in rat cortical neurons (K065). For a repressor whose baseline decay of the mRNA is ρ times the intrinsic decay, removing all of it raises the level by 1 + ρ, and a blockade cannot be more than complete, so ρ is at least 0.44 for that pair. That is a floor and not an estimate: the blockade was probably partial, the readout is a reporter, and two microRNAs are lumped, so it says nothing about miR-7 alone. The declared scan of k_repress (0.1 to 3) puts ρ between 0.94 and 28 in the PD arm and between 6.6 and 198 in the AD arm. The PD scenarios are therefore also run at ρ = 0.44, and a separate sweep runs every scenario at 24 h over ρ from 0.01 to 10⁴ and keeps, for each, the smallest peak over all ρ, a bound that holds whatever ρ is. Weaker repression raises every dose roughly in proportion to 1/ρ, and stronger repression approaches a limit. A second, smaller result comes from the measured reaction orders of Aβ42 nucleation (K013 primary, K012 secondary, both 2): the ratio of two nucleation rates at two monomer levels needs the orders and nothing else, so the AD over control monomer ratio of 1.41 corresponds to a nucleation rate ratio of 1.995, and offsetting the clearance deficit to a 49.9% fall in the nucleation rate, without any of the Aβ42 rate constants that no source gives (K037). That applies in vitro orders to a brain monomer pool and is a scaling, not a rate.

Dosed once a day, a miR-7 mimic that turns over like endogenous miR-7 (half-life 1.7 h, K020) must peak at **9.79×** its average level, against **1.26×** for a miRNA of median stability (34 h, K016) — a **7.7-fold** penalty in the peak needed to hold the same average. It spends **7.1%** of each day above half its own peak. To be dosed daily on the terms an ordinary miRNA enjoys, it would need roughly **20-fold** stabilisation (1.7 h → 34 h); at its measured half-life the equivalent interval is **1.2 h**. Under the upper-bound miR-7 half-life (5 h, K021) the requirement falls to 6.8-fold, and for the miR-29 paralogues to 4.9-fold (miR-29b) and 3.2-fold (miR-29c). The result is stated as a required fold stabilisation rather than as a verdict, because a chemically stabilised mimic is by construction not cleared at the endogenous rate; it is a design target the measurements set, and it is the quantity a delivery chemistry has to beat.

**A graphical abstract from the same numbers.** `scripts/18_graphical_abstract.py` draws one schematic figure spanning both axes, for a reader who wants the mechanism before the results section. Every number placed on it - the AD/control ratio, the BACE1:APP stoichiometry, the required knockdown, the elongation slowdown, the dosing penalty - is loaded at draw time from the same JSON and CSV files `scripts/08` already checks, and a manifest (`results/tables/graphical_abstract_values.json`) records what was drawn. The Argonaute2 inset reuses `scripts/13`'s own render (PDB 6N4O) rather than a second, unverified drawing of the same molecule; because that structure carries human AGO2 loaded with miR-122, not miR-29 or miR-7, the caption says so rather than implying otherwise.

**Figures of the mechanistic study.** `scripts/38_mechanistic_figures.py` draws the seven main-text figures of the mechanistic study (the structure of the two models beside four deposited structures; where each row of the parameter table was measured and what it feeds; the Alzheimer's arm; the Parkinson's arm; the turnover of the delivered microRNA; the response at the target; the design space). Every number on a figure is read at draw time from the result tables of `scripts/12`, `17`, `36` and `40`, from `scripts/_provenance.py` and from the parameter table, or follows from a closed form written beside the line it draws; where the geometry of a drawing carries data (the tanks of Fig. 3, the bands of Fig. 2) the sizes are computed from the table the figure is about. The one simulation, the time course of Fig. 6a, re-runs the cascade of `scripts/36` at the dose that script reports. The molecular images are the renders of `scripts/39` and are illustration. The schematic checks, from the rendered text extents, that no label runs over its box, and `scripts/_journal_fit.py` fits all seven figures to the journal's artwork limits. The ordinal teal ramp that shades the systems of Fig. 2 was checked with the data-visualisation palette validator (one hue, lightness that rises at every step, visible step gaps, a light end that clears the white surface).

**The molecular renders.** `scripts/39_structure_renders_article2.py` renders, with the open-source build of PyMOL (headless ray tracing, 2400 x 1800 px, transparent background), the four structures that `scripts/13` already reads: human Argonaute2 with a guide and a target (6N4O), BACE1 with an inhibitor (4D8C), the Abeta(1-42) fibril (5OQV) and the rod polymorph of the alpha-synuclein fibril (6CU7). Title, method and resolution are read from `results/tables/structure_figure_provenance.json`. Two constructions are not in the deposited coordinates, and the figure legends name them: (i) in the side views of the fibrils, the translucent layer past the last chain is that chain moved by the rigid-body transformation (a Kabsch superposition on the C-alpha atoms) that carries the previous chain of the same protofilament onto it, and the script records the rise, the rotation, the number of C-alpha pairs and the rms of each fit in `results/tables/structure_renders_article2.json` (`scripts/08` requires a fit below 0.05 A over at least 40 pairs); (ii) the BACE1 surface is cut by a clipping plane so that the inhibitor and the catalytic aspartates can be seen. No number of any result is read from an image.

**The design space.** `scripts/40_mimic_design_space.py` solves, with the solver, the constants and the dose cap of `scripts/36`, the dose that brings the cycle-averaged protein down by the target on a grid of 49 values of rho (0.01 to 10^4, eight per decade) and 19 dosing intervals (2 to 168 h), for nine series (three delivered species for each target: PD at 30% and at 43%, AD at the knockdown that the clearance deficit requires), 8,379 points in all. It writes `results/tables/mimic_design_space.csv` and `.json` with the peak of the repressor pool over its baseline and, for each series, the longest interval within a peak of 3, 10 and 30 times baseline (linear interpolation of the log peak against the log interval) at the low, middle and high ends of the declared scan of rho and at the K065 floor. Its checks: the grid reproduces the scenarios of `scripts/36` at 24 h (63 points, relative difference 6 x 10^-10), the peak never falls as the interval grows and no point is reached after one that is not, and the shortest unreached interval agrees with the closed form within the stated bound. The three ceilings are round reference points and are not safety limits.

**Where each number was measured.** `scripts/_provenance.py` declares, as one list of parameter identifiers per class, the system in which each of the 67 rows of the parameter table was measured (people in vivo; post-mortem human brain; rodent brain; primary neurons; cell lines and fibroblasts; cell-free recombinant protein; no measurement) and the block of the models that it feeds (miRNA pool and turnover; mRNA turnover; repression strength; protein turnover and abundance; Abeta42 production and clearance; aggregation kinetics). The module stops if a row is listed twice or is missing, or if the declared system disagrees with what the row says about species, population, condition and method. It writes `results/tables/parameter_provenance_classes.csv`, which `scripts/08` compares with the classification and `scripts/37` counts for the manuscript.

**Animating the same simulations.** `scripts/19_mechanism_animations.py` produces three GIFs, and reuses rather than re-derives: it imports the ODE right-hand sides and measured constants directly from `scripts/12`, and the closed-form dosing formula from `scripts/17`, so an animation cannot compute a different number from the one already checked. (i) Aβ42 monomer accumulating to its new steady state, control versus AD — no free parameter, since production and clearance are both measured. (ii) A single 10-fold mimic bolus washing out at the measured half-life, miR-7 against a median-stability miRNA. (iii) The repeated-dosing sawtooth: concentration relative to its own average, over four daily doses, for the same two species, so the peak height read directly off the axis is the peak-to-average penalty. Each GIF's final frame is written to `results/tables/mechanism_animations.json`, and `scripts/08` checks it against `ode_calibrated_results.json` and `mimic_dosing_feasibility.json` rather than trusting that the animation and the static figure agree. The α-synuclein pH-gate fibril growth is deliberately not animated: its acidic-pH rate constant is illustrative (K042), and animating it would put a specific speed on screen for a quantity this project reports only as a direction.

**Structural figures.** `scripts/13_structure_figures.py` renders four deposited structures with PyMOL: human Argonaute2 with a guide and target (6N4O), BACE1 with a bound inhibitor (4D8C), a full-length α-synuclein fibril (6CU7) and an Aβ(1-42) fibril (5OQV). Title, method, resolution and primary citation are read from each file, and the script stops if the title does not match the molecule the figure claims to show. The BACE1 catalytic aspartates are found twice, by sequence motif and by distance to the inhibitor, and must agree. Fibril protofilaments are assigned from the coordinates as chains stacked at the cross-β spacing. The figures illustrate mechanism; they are not a result.

**One composite panel, not four loose images.** `scripts/20_structure_story_panel.py` arranges four of `scripts/13`'s own PNGs into a single figure, captioned entirely from `results/tables/structure_figure_provenance.json` - no PyMOL session is reopened and no new claim is made. Panels are grouped by which axis they belong to with a coloured accent bar (blue for the Alzheimer's axis, orange for Parkinson's, grey for what both axes share), not chained with arrows: BACE1 does not structurally become the Aβ fibril, and no arrow claims it does.

**Ranking the series.** `scripts/46_mimic_series_ranking.py` answers which of the nine series holds a target down for the longest dosing interval, with a criterion fixed after the design space of `scripts/40` was computed and before the ranking was, and built only from quantities that `scripts/40` defines (the design space already showed that the more stable species allows the longer interval, so the ranking confirms that pattern and does not discover it): for each series, target knockdown and anchor of the repression strength (the low, midpoint and high ends of the scan, and the floor set by K065), the longest interval at which the repressor peak needed stays within 3, 10 or 30 times the baseline pool. The primary reading is 10 times at the midpoint. Series are ranked among the measured species; the series labelled median miRNA is a reference of median stability, not a mimic that was studied, and is ranked apart. Every combination is ranked and listed in `results/tables/mimic_series_ranking.csv`, and the JSON records whether first place changes with the threshold, the anchor or the target, and how many combinations tie (one of the 33: the 43% target at the floor of the repression strength and a 3-fold ceiling, where no series qualifies and all are at 0 h). Intervals quoted in the manuscript are rounded to the nearest value, one decimal below 10 h and whole hours from 10 h, as in the figures. A figure features the first place of the primary reading and shows all the other series beside it.

## 2. Figures in two languages

Every figure in `results/figures/` is emitted twice, once in English and once in Brazilian Portuguese, as `<name>.en.png` and `<name>.pt-BR.png`. The two versions are produced by the same code path in the same run from the same arrays: `scripts/_bilingual.py` exposes the language list, a `t(lang, en, pt)` chooser for label text and a path builder, and each plotting function is called once per language. Only words are translated. Numbers, axis limits, tick positions and the data themselves are identical by construction, because they are computed before the language loop begins and are not passed through the translator — a figure pair cannot disagree about a value without the code that drew it having changed.
